Adversarial cognitive mirror

The product's differentiator: it audits itself.

Most profiling tools try to produce a convincing answer. The stronger idea is a system that asks: how much of this answer survives stress tests?

Example: one pattern, competing explanations

Observed pattern: a writer becomes much more concise and declarative during conflict.

  • H1: Control strategy — narrowing the space for disagreement.
  • H2: Cognitive load — conflict reduces available attention for elaboration.
  • H3: Boundary setting — the writer is communicating a firm limit.
  • H4: Context artifact — the messages are shorter because of time pressure or device conditions.

The system then asks: does the same pattern appear in non-conflict conditions under time pressure? Does it occur when the writer is setting boundaries without hostility? What evidence would make each hypothesis less plausible?

System audit

Challenge the analyst, data, signal, and conclusion.

Challenge the analyst

Are we selecting examples that support the conclusion while ignoring contradictions? Are we leaning on emotionally salient messages?

Challenge the data

Is the sample large enough, comparable in genre, and representative of the relevant context?

Challenge the signal

Could the pattern instead reflect topic, genre, formatting, translation effects, or editing history rather than authorial style?

Challenge the conclusion

A strong stylometric match is not proof of identity. It is one piece of evidence that requires verification, calibration, and skepticism.

Profile the relationship

Not just the writer—analyze the interaction system.

Control of framing

Who establishes definitions, constraints, and standards used to judge the exchange?

Burden of justification

Who must repeatedly explain or defend their position? Are comparable claims treated symmetrically?

Option-space change

Does the exchange expand, preserve, or narrow what each participant can reasonably do or say?

Reciprocity

Are standards of evidence, accountability, and revision applied evenly across participants?

Counterevidence
