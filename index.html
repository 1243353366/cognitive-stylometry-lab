Anti-confirmation bias

Challenge the person, the method, and the conclusion.

Challenge the analyst

Are we selecting examples that support the conclusion? Are we ignoring the documents that weaken it?

Challenge the data

Is the sample large enough? Is it comparable in genre, topic, purpose, and context?

Challenge the signal

Could the pattern reflect topic, genre, formatting, or translation effects instead of authorial style?

Challenge the conclusion

A strong stylometric match is not proof of identity. It is one piece of evidence that requires context, verification, and skepticism.

The principle

Style can be informative without being decisive.

Computational stylometry is useful for comparing writing habits, structure, and rhetorical patterns. But style is never a complete explanation. Genre, topic, context, and editing history can all shape the same signal.

The right workflow is not “find the match.” It is “test competing explanations.” That is the difference between evidence and overconfidence.
